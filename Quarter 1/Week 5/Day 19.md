# Day 19: First Great Awakening and the Enlightenment

You've now learned a little about each of the 13 original colonies. These would eventually become states, but before we get to that, today and tomorrow we'll take a look at some of the major events that shaped and unified the colonies for what would lie ahead. 
  
Start by reading the textbook sections entitled "The First Great Awakening" and "The Enlightenment": [Section 4.4: Great Awakening and Enlightenment](https://openstax.org/books/us-history/pages/4-4-great-awakening-and-enlightenment)

The **First Great Awakening** was a massive revival led by preachers such as Theodorus Frelinghuysen, Gilbert Tennent, Jonathan Edwards, George Whitefield. Tens of thousands of colonists became believers or became more dedicated in their walks with Christ. As a result, the culture in which America would be founded was largely influenced by Christian values. Additionally, faith in Christ united diverse colonists who came from various regions, professions, and ethnic backgrounds.

While the First Great Awakening shaped the spiritual hearts of many colonists, the **Enlightenment** was an intellectual movement. Like the First Great Awakening, the Enlightenment began in England before spreading to the English colonies in the New World. The Enlightenment produced several modern ideas about philosophy, science, and politics. Particularly, some of the political ideas from the Enlightenment would challenge the traditional European monarchies. 

Watch the following video to learn more about the First Great Awakening (and a little more about the Enlightenment): ["The Great Awakening Explained | America's First Religious Revival](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PuJPYmZVeTM)
