# Day 21: Growing Tension in the Colonies

After the French and Indian War, Britain found itself deeply in debt. To attempt to offset this debt, the British Parliament passed a number of acts that taxed the colonies:
* Sugar Act (1764) - a tariff (tax on imported goods) on sugar brought into the colonies
* Stamp Act (1765) - a charge for official seals/stamps needed on all printed materials (e.g., newspapers, legal documents, etc.)
* Townsend Acts (1767-1768) - tariffs on a wide range of goods such as paper, glass, and tea
* Tea Act (1773) - a reinforcement of the tax on tea

England's lack of hands-on governing in the colonies fostered a sense of independence among many colonists. As a result, colonists grew resentful of the taxes, and phrases such as **"No taxation without representation"** (meaning, the colonists had no representatives in the British Parliament, where the tax acts were being made into law) began to circle. 

The resentment turned into protest, and in 1770, the protest boiled into turmoil with the **Boston Massacre**. Read the section of the textbook titled "Trouble in Boston" to find out what the Boston Massacre was: [Section 5.3 The Townsend Acts and Colonial Protest](https://openstax.org/books/us-history/pages/5-3-the-townshend-acts-and-colonial-protest).

To add to the chaos, after the passing of the Tea Act, members of the Sons of Liberty (a group of Bostonians who led the protest against what they viewed as the British government taking away their rights) dressed up as Native Americans boarded a British tea ship and dumped all of the tea on board into the Boston Harbor. This event became known as the **Boston Tea Party**.

In response to the Boston Tea Party, the British Parliament passed the Coercive Acts (also known as the Intolerable Acts) which forced the colonists to house British soldiers and shut down Boston Harbor. 

### Key Concepts to Remember:
* "No taxation without representation"
* Boston Massacre
* Boston Tea Party
