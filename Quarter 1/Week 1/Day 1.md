# Day 1: Introduction

What is history? Why is it important to know where you came from? Why study American history specifically?

Well, for starters, history is simply a story of the past. It's how things began, how they changed, and sometimes, how they ended. However, not every story is over yet, as is the case of the United States. We can study the history of our country to find out how we ended up here, and we can learn to make better decisions for the next part of the story.

Watch this video on why it's important to study history: </br>
[Why Study History?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rCrASZ2zd8c)

Now that we know why it's important to study history, let's start our story. Every story has a beginning. Pause and answer: what do you think the beginning of our country's story is?

I propose we start *way* back and see how people came to live here to begin with. Many scholars believe that when people were first created, they lived in what is considered the Middle East today. Then how did those people get all the way around the world? Watch this video to find out what some scholars think: </br>
[Beringia: The Gateway Between Worlds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kd00htzaHYw&t=95s)

Also, maybe when you hear Ice Age, you might be skeptical. Many evolutionists talk about the earth being millions of years old with multiples Ice Ages that lasted thousands of years each. However, did you know many creationists do believe that the Flood from the book of Genesis could have caused an Ice Age? Read this page and watch the video at the top of it: </br>
